About the NTU Multilingual Corpus

Overview

The NTU Multilingual Corpus (NTUMC) is an open, semantically annotated multilingual corpus originally developed at Nanyang Technological University (NTU), Singapore, and now maintained by the Computational Linguistics Laboratory (凡土研) at Palacký University Olomouc. It contains literary texts (short stories, essays), newspaper text, and tourism descriptions in multiple languages, with all texts linked at the sentence level and annotated with word-sense tags from the Open Multilingual Wordnet.

Languages

  • Chinese — 282 documents, 7,094 sentences
  • English — 280 documents, 10,930 sentences
  • Indonesian — 225 documents, 3,053 sentences
  • Japanese — 6 documents, 4,256 sentences
  • Czech — 1 document, 6,047 sentences
  • Italian — 1 document, 608 sentences

Annotation

Every word in the corpus is lemmatised and tagged with a part of speech. Content words are linked to synsets in the Open Multilingual Wordnet (OMW), which is itself linked to the Princeton WordNet. Named entities are classified as person, organisation, number, date, or other.

Hover or click any word in a document page to see its lemma, part of speech, WordNet definition, and synonyms. Sentence-level translations are available between aligned language pairs.

The % tagged shown on the document index is the proportion of taggable concepts that have been assigned a sense tag: |tagged| / |taggable|, where tagged concepts have a real synset or named-entity tag (excluding e, w, x, and empty), and taggable concepts are all identified concepts minus those marked x (should not be tagged). This measures annotation completeness over open-class words rather than over all tokens.

Data Format

Each document is a static HTML page with word-level annotations embedded as <w> elements carrying data-p (POS), data-l (lemma), and data-c (concept IDs) attributes. Shared data files in data/ provide WordNet definitions, synonyms, and sentence alignments as JSON, loaded on demand.

Use the button in the toolbar on any document page to download its annotations as a TSV file.
